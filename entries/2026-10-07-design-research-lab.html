Design Research Methods Lab: The Bakery

October 7, 2026 · Week 6 · In-class lab with Vida and Kaisha

In this lab, we practiced user and stakeholder interviews. Each team picked a scenario, wrote interview guides, and interviewed synthetic participants: an AI playing one person in the case. My team (Vida, Kaisha, and I) chose the bakery.

The scenario

A neighborhood bakery opens early and usually closes by mid-afternoon. Customers periodically ask why it is not open after work. The bakery makes products on site, sells directly to walk-in customers, and also supplies other businesses. The owner is interested in whether later hours could work, but does not want to simply keep the current staff working longer.

Research question: What currently determines the bakery's hours, and what conditions would make a later production or retail window viable?

The method

An interview guide is one page of questions written ahead of time, in five parts: an introduction, two warm-up questions, four to six story questions ("Tell me about…", "Walk me through…"), one "show me" task, and a close. The point is that analytics shows what people did, but an interview tells you why they did it.

Each of us interviewed one person who works at the bakery and then one customer as a follow-up.

My questions

Cashier

  1. How often do you work at the bakery? How long is your work shift, and how long has it been since you started working here?
  2. What are your roles and responsibilities other than cashier? Do you usually help with closing procedures?
  3. How do you feel about changing the opening hours? Do you think it will affect your working routine?
  4. Are you able to work longer if the owner asks?

Customer (businessman)

  1. How often do you go to the bakery, and what time do you usually go? Do you have a favorite bread? How much is it?
  2. Do you think you would be grateful if the bakery opening hours were extended?
  3. Are there a lot of other customers when you go to the bakery? Also, are there still many types of bread left?

Affinity map

After the interviews, I wrote each finding as one note, tagged with who said it, and grouped similar notes into clusters.

The day is front-loaded

  • OwnerThe rush is 7 to 9:30 a.m., with a smaller wave around lunch.
  • OwnerWholesale orders go out on the van before the shop opens.
  • OwnerBy the 2 p.m. close, the cases are often nearly empty.
  • KenComes 8 to 9 a.m. The sourdough is sometimes down to a few loaves by 9.

Staff are at the end of a long day

  • OwnerThe head baker starts at 3 a.m. and has been on his feet nine hours by noon.
  • OwnerPaying overtime for a slow afternoon does not make sense.
  • BakerWould not stay until 6 p.m., even for more pay.
  • BakerA separate later shift “would feel different.”
  • CashierCan stay an extra hour or two with notice, but not regularly.
  • CashierWants a predictable schedule.

Freshness and waste

  • OwnerA croissant baked at 5 a.m. is not good at 5 p.m.
  • OwnerBread holds up. Sourdough is fine at 6 p.m.
  • OwnerButter is the most expensive ingredient. Unsold pastries are wasted money.
  • BakerHard to guess how much to bake for an afternoon crowd.

Demand is real, but occasional

  • EthanWants to stop by after class around 4:30 or 5, but it is closed.
  • EthanWould use later hours for a snack or coffee, not dinner.
  • KenWould be glad of later hours, but would not use them every day.
  • OwnerMost requests come from the new apartments nearby, for bread to take home for dinner.
  • Owner“Saying it isn’t the same as showing up at 5:30 on a Tuesday.”

The space is small

  • OwnerThe front is about 400 square feet: four stools at the window and one small table.
  • OwnerNo customer bathroom.
  • OwnerIn the afternoon, the kitchen is full of prep for the next morning.
  • OwnerDoes not want to become a study café.

Reflection

The interviews showed that the question is not just about hours. Each person saw a different part of the problem. The owner was thinking about freshness, cost, and reputation. The baker and cashier were thinking about their own schedules. The customers were thinking about convenience. Interviewing different roles and putting their answers side by side made the constraints much clearer than any one interview could. A later window looks possible only with a separate afternoon shift and products that hold up well, like bread.

Looking back at my own questions, a few were yes/no questions, like "Do you think you would be grateful if the bakery opening hours were extended?" The guide suggests asking about specific stories instead, like "Tell me about the last time you wanted to go to the bakery and it was closed." That kind of question would probably have given me more concrete answers. I also did not include a "show me" task. Next time, I want to write more story questions and fewer questions that suggest the answer.

The participants in this lab were synthetic: an AI played each role from a shared prompt. The answers are useful practice, but they are not real user research.

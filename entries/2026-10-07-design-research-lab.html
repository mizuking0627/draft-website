Design Research Methods Lab: The Bakery

October 7, 2026 · Week 6 · In-class lab with Vida and Kaisha

In this lab, we practiced user and stakeholder interviews. Each team picked a scenario, wrote interview guides, and interviewed synthetic participants: an AI playing one person in the case. My team (Vida, Kaisha, and I) chose the bakery.

The scenario

A neighborhood bakery opens early and usually closes by mid-afternoon. Customers periodically ask why it is not open after work. The bakery makes products on site, sells directly to walk-in customers, and also supplies other businesses. The owner is interested in whether later hours could work, but does not want to simply keep the current staff working longer.

Research question: What currently determines the bakery's hours, and what conditions would make a later production or retail window viable?

What we did

Each of us came up with questions for one person at the bakery. Then we pasted the interview prompt into an AI chat, so the AI would answer as that person, and asked our questions. After that, we asked follow-up questions, either to the same person or to a customer.

My questions

Cashier

  1. How often do you work at the bakery? How long is your work shift, and how long has it been since you started working here?
  2. What are your roles and responsibilities other than cashier? Do you usually help with closing procedures?
  3. How do you feel about changing the opening hours? Do you think it will affect your working routine?
  4. Are you able to work longer if the owner asks?

Customer (businessman)

  1. How often do you go to the bakery, and what time do you usually go? Do you have a favorite bread? How much is it?
  2. Do you think you would be grateful if the bakery opening hours were extended?
  3. Are there a lot of other customers when you go to the bakery? Also, are there still many types of bread left?

Affinity map: what we learned

We grouped the answers from all five interviews by topic.

1. The bakery is busiest in the morning

  • Owner: Most sales happen from 7 to 9:30 a.m. By the 2 p.m. close, there is not much left.
  • Ken (customer): He comes around 8 or 9 a.m., and his favorite sourdough is sometimes almost sold out.

2. Staff do not want longer days

  • Baker: He starts before 5 a.m. and would not want to stay until the evening. A separate later shift would be OK.
  • Cashier: She can sometimes stay an extra hour or two, but not every day.
  • Owner: She does not want to overwork her staff or pay overtime for slow afternoons.

3. The food may not stay fresh

  • Owner: Croissants made early in the morning are not good by the evening. Bread lasts longer.
  • Baker: It is hard to know how much to bake for later customers, so food could go to waste.

4. Customers would come, but not every day

  • Ethan (customer): He would stop by after class for a snack or coffee, but not for dinner.
  • Ken (customer): He would like later hours, but would not use them every day.
  • Owner: Many requests come from people in the new apartments nearby who want bread for dinner.

5. The shop is small

  • Owner: There are only a few seats and no customer bathroom, so it cannot be a study spot.

Reflection

The interviews showed that the question is not just about hours. Each person saw a different part of the problem. The owner was thinking about freshness, cost, and reputation. The baker and cashier were thinking about their own schedules. The customers were thinking about convenience. Putting all the answers together made the problem much clearer than any one interview could. Later hours look possible only with a separate afternoon shift and products that stay fresh, like bread.

Looking back at my own questions, some of them could be answered with just yes or no, like "Do you think you would be grateful if the bakery opening hours were extended?" Questions that ask for a specific story, like "Tell me about the last time you wanted to visit the bakery and it was closed," would probably have given more detailed answers. Next time, I want to ask more questions like that.

The people in these interviews were played by an AI, not real people. The answers were good practice, but they are not real user research.
